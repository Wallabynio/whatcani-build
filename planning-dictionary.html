B

BASIXBuilding Sustainability Index

NSW’s online assessment that sets energy, water and thermal comfort targets for new homes and many alterations. A BASIX certificate is lodged with the DA or CDC.

Building envelope

The three-dimensional space a building has to fit within, set by controls such as height, setbacks and FSR.

C

CDCComplying Development Certificate

A fast-track approval for development that fully meets set standards, issued by a council or a registered certifier instead of going through a development application.

Read: DA vs CDC →
Clause 4.6Exceptions to development standards

The clause in a council’s LEP that lets the consent authority approve a building that breaches a development standard, such as height or FSR, when a written request justifies it.

Construction certificate

Confirms that the building plans meet the Building Code and the conditions of the development consent. Needed before building work starts under a DA.

D

DADevelopment Application

An application to council, or another consent authority, for approval to develop. It is assessed on its merits against the LEP, the DCP and other planning rules.

Read: DA vs CDC →
DCPDevelopment Control Plan

The council’s detailed design rules, such as setbacks, landscaping and parking. More flexible than the LEP.

Dual occupancy

Two dwellings on one lot, attached or detached. Whether it is allowed depends on the zone and the council’s LEP.

F

FSRFloor space ratio

The total floor area of a building divided by the area of its site. An FSR of 0.5:1 on a 600 m² lot allows about 300 m² of floor area.

H

Heritage conservation area

An area listed in the LEP for its historic character. Changes usually need a DA and have to respect that character.

Housing SEPPState Environmental Planning Policy (Housing) 2021

The state policy covering housing types such as secondary dwellings, affordable housing, seniors housing and low-rise housing.

L

Landscaped area

The part of a site kept for planting rather than paving or building. DCPs and codes set a minimum share of the site.

LEPLocal Environmental Plan

The council’s main planning instrument. It sets the zone, what can be built in it, and controls such as height and FSR.

M

Minimum lot size

The smallest lot area the LEP allows when land is subdivided. Some councils set a separate minimum for dual occupancies.

P

Planning certificateSection 10.7 certificate

A certificate from council listing the planning controls and constraints that apply to a property, such as zoning, heritage and flooding.

R

R2 Low Density Residential

A zone mainly for detached houses. What else it permits depends on the council’s LEP.

Read: what zoning R2 means for your site →
R3 Medium Density Residential

A zone for a mix of housing types, such as townhouses, villas and small apartment buildings.

S

SEEStatement of Environmental Effects

The report lodged with a DA that explains the proposal, its likely impacts and how it meets the planning rules.

Secondary dwellingGranny flat

A self-contained home on the same lot as the main house. The rules are in the Housing SEPP.

SEPPState Environmental Planning Policy

Planning rules made by the NSW Government that apply across the state or to particular areas, and can override a council’s LEP.

Setback

The distance a building has to be from a boundary. Front, side and rear setbacks are usually set by the DCP or a code.

Definitions are a plain-language guide, not advice for a particular site. The controls that apply to your land are in your council’s LEP and DCP and your planning certificate.
